## Part 1 — Review: what the Frase AI writer actually is

Most AI writers start from a prompt. Frase starts from **research**. Before it writes a word, it pulls keywords, the pages that rank, the questions people ask, content gaps competitors miss, and citable statistics. You choose which of those to keep, and the writer builds the article around them.

That design matters for SEO. A prompt-only tool writes what sounds plausible; Frase writes what the search results show readers want, with sources attached.

### What we liked

- **Speed with substance.** Research took about 4–5 minutes, the brief under a minute, and the ~1,500-word draft about 3–4 minutes.
- **Structure is excellent.** Numbered H2s, a comparison table, "Best For" callouts and a Bottom Line section came out of the box. That is why the GEO score hit 94%.
- **Sources are visible.** The brief lists key facts with a ✓ Verified badge and the domain they came from, so you can check them before they reach the draft.
- **It reads human-ish.** Our draft opened with a concrete scene rather than "In today's fast-paced world".
- **Scores tell you what to fix,** split into SEO, GEO and EEAT, with tips for each.

### What we didn't like

- **EEAT is weak out of the box (33%).** AI can't add your photos, tests or credentials. Experience scored 5% and Authority 0%.
- **Credits go fast.** Each draft uses one article. "Improve scores" and "Rewrite" each cost one more article too.
- **Product claims need checking.** The writer is only as accurate as its sources; verify specs and prices yourself.
- **Small quirks.** While finishing, the structure panel listed some headings twice; check for duplicates before publishing.
- **Language defaults to your browser.** If your browser isn't in English, switch the content language before running research.

## Part 2 — How to write an SEO article with AI: research first

Now the guide. This is the exact workflow we used, and it works on the free trial.

**Step 1: Start a new research.** Go to **Plan & Create → Research → + New Research**. Enter your topic or target keyword.

**Step 2: Check language and market.** Set the content language to **English (US)** and the target market to your country. This is the most common mistake for non-US users.

**Step 3: Add a goal and audience.** One line each, e.g. "Goal: help readers pick a tool. Audience: freelance bloggers on a budget." The brief and draft get noticeably more specific.

**Step 4: Run research and wait 4–5 minutes.** Frase runs keywords, SERP analysis, gaps and evidence together.

![Frase research running keywords, SERP, content gaps and evidence at the same time](/img/frase/12_research_running.jpg)

**Step 5: Pin what you want.** The results have four tabs: Keywords, Headings, Questions and Evidence. Pin:

- 1 primary keyword and 2–4 close variants;
- 5–8 headings from top-ranking pages you want to cover;
- 3–5 real questions from Reddit and People Also Ask (these become your FAQ);
- 3–5 statistics with named sources (AI answer engines love quoting these).

The **Opportunities** section is the most valuable part: angles the ranking pages miss. Pin at least one; it becomes your reason to exist.

![Pinning keywords, headings, questions and evidence before generating the brief](/img/frase/14_pin_inputs.jpg)

## Part 3 — Build the brief (your AI outline)

**Step 6: Click Generate brief and choose a content type.** Frase offers 18 types. For SEO and affiliate content, these map neatly:

| You're writing | Choose |
|---|---|
| "Best X for Y" | Listicle |
| "X vs Y" | Comparison Article |
| "X review" | Tool Review |
| "X alternatives" | Alternatives |
| "How to X" | How-To or Tutorial |

**Step 7: Set the word count.** Default is 1,400. Match the top results, which Frase shows in the score panel later, rather than guessing.

**Step 8: Edit the brief before writing.** This is the cheapest place to fix the article. Check:

- **Key facts:** approve ✓ or reject ✕ each one; add your own sources.
- **Core argument:** one sentence your article proves. Rewrite it if it's generic.
- **What makes this different:** make sure your pinned opportunity is here.
- **Outline:** reorder sections, delete fluff, and mark sections you'll write yourself.

![A Frase brief outline with section goals, word targets and evidence to include](/img/frase/17_brief_outline.jpg)

## Part 4 — Let the AI write the draft

**Step 9: Click Generate article, then assign sections.** In the setup step you choose, per section, "Frase writes" or "I write". Our tip: let Frase write the informational sections and **keep the verdict, personal-experience and recommendation sections for yourself**. That is where EEAT comes from.

**Step 10: Click Write.** Frase writes section by section while the score updates live. Our draft took 3–4 minutes and used one article credit.

## Part 5 — Edit for EEAT and optimize SEO + GEO

This is the part that decides whether an AI article ranks. Open the **Score** panel. You get three sub-scores.

**SEO (ours: 78%).** Compares words, headings, keywords, meta tags and readability with the top results. Our quick wins were putting the primary keyword in the introduction and adding images, since the draft had none.

**GEO (ours: 94%).** How likely AI answers (ChatGPT, Google AI) are to quote you. To push it higher: turn vague prose into short factual statements, bold key definitions, and attribute every statistic.

**EEAT (ours: 33%).** The weak spot of every AI draft.

![Frase EEAT breakdown showing low Experience and Authority scores on the AI draft](/img/frase/22_eeat.jpg)

Fix EEAT by hand, not with credits:

1. **Complete your author profile** (bio, job title, 3+ expertise tags). Frase estimates up to +14 points.
2. **Add first-hand media:** your own screenshots, photos or test results. Experience was 5% without them.
3. **Write one paragraph only you could write:** what you tried, what surprised you, who you'd tell to skip it.
4. **Link to your social profiles and credentials** to lift Authority.
5. **Fact-check** every price, spec and claim against the official source.

**Avoid burning credits.** "Improve scores" and "Rewrite" each cost one article. On Starter (10 a month), editing EEAT yourself is cheaper and better.

**Final checklist before publishing:** no duplicate headings; 3+ internal links; images with descriptive alt text; meta title and description (Frase auto-writes them); a clear verdict near the top.

## After publishing: track AI search mentions

Once live, add a few prompts in **Monitor → AI Visibility**, such as "best AI SEO tool for freelancers". Frase shows whether ChatGPT or Google AI mentions you and **which sites those answers cite**. Those sites are your outreach list. Starter tracks ChatGPT and Google AI; higher plans add Perplexity, Claude and Gemini.

## FAQ

**Can Frase write a full SEO article by itself?**
Yes. It researched, outlined and drafted ~1,500 words in under 10 minutes in our test. You should still edit it for experience, accuracy and voice before publishing.

**Will Google penalize AI-written articles?**
Google's stated position is that it rewards helpful content regardless of how it is produced. Thin, unedited AI content is the risk, which is why the EEAT editing step matters.

**How many articles do I get on the trial?**
Our trial allowed 5 articles, along with research, audit and AI-visibility credits.

**Does "Improve scores" cost extra?**
It counts as one article from your monthly allowance, and so does "Rewrite".

**Can Frase write in other languages?**
Yes, but it picks the language from your browser by default. Set the content language deliberately before each research.
