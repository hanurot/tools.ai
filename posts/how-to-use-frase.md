## Step 1: Onboarding: let Frase read your site

After signing up you land on the Frase Agent's setup screen. Enter your website and click **Analyze My Website**.

![Frase Agent onboarding asks for your website](/img/frase/01_onboarding_enter_website.jpg)

**Gotcha:** Frase needs a **root domain** (example.com). We first tried a site hosted in a sub-folder (username.github.io/site) and Frase stripped the path, then failed with "couldn't read business information". If your blog lives in a sub-folder, connect the main domain instead.

While you wait, Frase maps your pages, searches your market and visits competitors' "About" pages. In our test it mapped 30 pages and found 10 topics in about two minutes.

![Frase researching the market and competitors](/img/frase/03_researching_market.jpg)

## Step 2: Review your brand profile

Frase then shows what it learned: a business overview, website type, value proposition, target audience (four personas in our case), brand values, tagline, and five "matching criteria" it uses to judge competitors. It also suggested three competitors to track.

![Review the brand profile Frase built](/img/frase/05_review_profile.jpg)

**Fix anything that's wrong now.** Every brief and article Frase writes later starts from this profile, so a wrong audience here means wrong articles later. You can edit it any time in **Brand Hub**, which also holds Brand Voice, Reference Docs, Templates and your Design System.

Finally, name the workspace and choose your first job: **Run a site audit** or **See AI visibility**.

![Name the workspace and pick a first job](/img/frase/06_name_workspace.jpg)

## Step 3: Find your way around the workspace

The sidebar is grouped by job:

- **Plan & Create:** Opportunities, Research, Content, Calendar

- **Publish:** Review queue, Conversions
- **Agent setup, Automations, Brand Hub, Settings**

The header shows how many articles you have left this month. The little mascot in the corner is the **Frase Agent**. You can ask it things like "audit my site" or "research a topic" from any page.

## Step 4: Run a Site Audit

Frase starts an audit automatically. It discovered 500+ URLs on our demo site and audited the first batch, ranking issues by impact. Typical findings: pages AI can't read, missing meta descriptions, thin pages, broken internal links, images without alt text.

![Site audit in progress](/img/frase/08_audit_running.jpg)

**Budget tip:** audit pages are a monthly allowance (50 on Starter). Audit your money pages and top-traffic posts first, not tag and category pages.

## Step 5: Check your AI Visibility

This is the feature most competing tools don't have. Frase asks AI engines the questions your customers ask and reports:

- **Visibility:** the share of answers that mention you
- **Share of voice:** your mentions compared with your tracked competitors
- **Average position** and **sentiment**
- **Citation sources:** which websites the AI answers quote

![AI Visibility overview](/img/frase/09_ai_visibility.jpg)

The most useful part is **citation sources**. In our test, 93% of what the AI answers cited came from editorial sites (blogs, guides, news), and none of it came from the brand's own site. The **Recommendations** tab turns that into actions: "Get into the articles AI quotes (3 sites to pitch)" and "Be the first answer to 1 open question".

![AI Visibility recommendations](/img/frase/10_ai_recommendations.jpg)

## Step 6: Research a topic

Go to **Research → New Research** and describe what you're writing about.

![New research setup](/img/frase/11_research_setup.jpg)

**Gotcha:** Frase guessed our **content language and target market from the browser locale**. It picked Vietnamese/Vietnam even though the topic was in English. Always check both fields before clicking **Run research**.

Research took about five minutes. Frase runs keywords, the SERP, content gaps and evidence together, then shows five sections:

1. **Keywords** with monthly searches and CPC
2. **What ranks & headings:** each top result with its word count and authority
3. **Opportunities:** angles competitors miss, each with a "research basis". This was the most valuable part. For our sneaker topic it suggested a travel-readiness table, wet-weather performance and break-in times, none of which the top pages covered properly.
4. **Questions** from Reddit and "People Also Ask"
5. **Evidence & insights:** citable statistics with their sources

![Research results](/img/frase/13_research_results.jpg)

Pin what you want to use. You need at least one keyword before **Generate brief** unlocks, and the "Pin suggestions" buttons pin the recommended items in one click.

![Pin inputs, then generate the brief](/img/frase/14_pin_inputs.jpg)

## Step 7: Configure and review the brief

Pick one of **18 content types** and a target word count. For affiliate and review sites, the useful ones map exactly to the formats that convert: **Listicle** (best X), **Comparison Article** (X vs Y), **Alternatives**, **Tool Review** and **How-To/Tutorial**.

![Configure brief](/img/frase/15_configure_brief.jpg)

The brief arrived in under a minute and was more thorough than most human-written briefs we've seen:

- Title, word count, tone, reading level, **funnel stage** and audience personas
- **Key facts**, each marked verified or not, with its source and approve/reject buttons
- A **core argument**, plus "what the reader wants": what they're searching for, how they feel, and what they'll do next
- **What makes this different** from the current top results
- An **outline** where every H2 has a word budget, a format note (for example "2 paragraphs + 'Best For' callout"), talking points and the evidence to include

![Brief overview](/img/frase/16_brief_ready.jpg)

![Brief outline](/img/frase/17_brief_outline.jpg)

Edit anything here before writing, because changing the brief is free while regenerating the article is not.

## Step 8: Generate the article

Click **Generate article**. In the set-up step you can choose which sections **Frase writes** and which you'll write yourself. Then click **Write**.

![The Frase editor](/img/frase/18_editor_setup.jpg)

Frase wrote section by section, and the Checklist panel tracked sections, keyword coverage, word count, headers, links and images as it went. The whole draft took **about 3–4 minutes** and used **1 of the 5 trial articles**.

![Writing live](/img/frase/19_writing_live.jpg)

**Draft quality:** better than we expected. It opened with a concrete hook rather than a generic definition, and it included bolded statistics with source links, "Best For" and "Packing Score" callouts, a comparison table and a "Bottom Line" section. The prose was still a little smooth and same-y, and the product claims need fact-checking before you publish.

## Step 9: Read the three scores (this is where you add value)

The **Score** panel compares your draft with the top results (our 1,484 words against a 971-word average; 8 headers against 4) and breaks the overall score into three parts.

![Score panel](/img/frase/21_score_panel.jpg)

| Score | Our AI draft | What it measures | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **GEO** | 94% | Can AI engines extract and cite you? | Add a short glossary and attribute every statistic |
| **SEO** | 78% | Keywords, meta tags, structure, readability | Put the primary keyword in the intro and add images |
| **EEAT** | 33% | Experience, expertise, authority, trust | Complete your author profile, add original photos/tests, and link your credentials |

![EEAT breakdown](/img/frase/22_eeat.jpg)

The key lesson from the test: **the AI is already good at structure (GEO and SEO) but can't fake experience.** Its EEAT breakdown showed Experience at 5% ("original media") and Authority at 0% ("add social profiles and credentials"). Completing the author profile alone is worth up to 14 points. Put your editing time into real photos, your own test notes and a proper author bio.

![GEO breakdown](/img/frase/23_geo_score.jpg)

**Credit warning:** the **Improve scores** and **Rewrite** buttons each count once toward your monthly article allowance. On Starter (10 articles a month), fix the EEAT items by hand instead.

![Improve scores counts toward your allowance](/img/frase/24_improve_scores.jpg)

## Step 10: Compare against competitors

**Competitive Analysis** puts any page, yours or a URL you paste, against the pages outranking it, or against up to three competitor URLs you choose. It then lists what to add. For a review site, the best use is to compare your review with the #1 result for "[tool] review".

![Competitive analysis](/img/frase/25_competitive_setup.jpg)

## Step 11: Automations (only when you trust it)

Automations come pre-built and **every one starts in approval mode**:

- **Rules:** "AI Citation Ready", "Publish-Ready" (this one can block a publish), "Quality Baseline"
- **Playbooks:** Comparison Article, Listicle, Product Page, Quick Update, SEO Blog Post. Each one researches, writes a brief, drafts the piece and then pauses for your approval.

## FAQ

**Do I need a credit card for the trial?**
No. The trial runs for 7 days, and you only add billing if you choose a plan.

**Does generating a brief use an article credit?**
In our test it didn't. Our counter only dropped (5 → 4) when we generated the article itself. "Improve scores" and "Rewrite" each count as one article.

**Can Frase publish to WordPress?**
Yes. It publishes to WordPress, Webflow, Sanity, Wix and Frase's own CMS. Auto-publishing is off by default and limited by plan.

**Is the AI draft ready to publish?**
Not as-is. It was well structured and well sourced, but you should fact-check the product claims and add your own experience. That's also what lifts the EEAT score.
