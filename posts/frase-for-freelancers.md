## How Frase fits a freelance writing workflow

A typical freelance SEO job has four stages. Frase covers the first three:

1. **Research** (about 4–5 minutes in our test). You enter a topic, language and target market. Frase returns recommended keywords with search volume and CPC, the top-ranking pages with word counts, content gaps competitors miss, audience questions from Reddit and People Also Ask, and citable statistics with named sources.
2. **Brief** (under a minute). You pin the inputs you want and pick a content type, such as Comparison Article, Alternatives, Tool Review or Listicle. Frase then builds a brief with:
    - a title
    - a "core argument"
    - what the reader wants
    - an angle that's different from the current results
    - key facts with ✓ Verified badges and source domains
    - a section-by-section outline with word budgets
3. **Draft** (about 3–4 minutes for ~1,500 words). Frase writes section by section. You can assign some sections to yourself and let Frase write the others.
4. **Your edit.** This is where you earn your rate.

![Frase content brief ready, showing article details, key facts with verified badges and the outline](/img/frase/16_brief_ready.jpg)

For a freelancer, the **brief is the most valuable part**. The "Opportunities" section in research surfaces specific angles the top results skip. That gives you something to tell a client beyond "I covered the keywords." You can also export or share a brief, which helps when a client wants to approve the outline before you write.

## The scores: strong structure, weak expertise

Our AI draft scored **94% on GEO** (AI-search readiness) and **78% on SEO**, but only **33% on E-E-A-T**.

The E-E-A-T breakdown explains why:

- **Experience: 5%.** The draft had no original media.
- **Authority: 0%.** No author credentials or social profiles were attached.
- **Author profile: 13% complete.** Frase estimates that finishing the profile adds up to 14 points.

![Frase E-E-A-T score panel showing low Experience and Authority sub-scores](/img/frase/22_eeat.jpg)

For freelancers this is good news and bad news.

- **The bad news:** you can't sell the raw draft as finished work. It needs fact-checking, first-hand detail, images and links. Our draft had zero images and only three links.
- **The good news:** that gap is exactly your value. Frase handles structure, coverage and quotable formatting, so your hours go into experience, interviews, examples and voice. Those are the things AI can't fake and clients notice.

One budget warning: Frase's **"Improve scores" and "Rewrite" each use one article credit** from your monthly allowance. On Starter's 10 articles, don't spend credits fixing E-E-A-T. Fix it by hand.

## The limit freelancers hit first: domains

Frase is built around a **site**. During onboarding it reads a root domain and builds a Brand Hub: positioning, audience personas, brand values and competitors. It then uses that profile in what it writes. Site audits and AI-visibility tracking are also tied to that site.

Starter includes **one domain**. Here's what that means in practice:

- If you write mostly for **your own blog or one retainer client**, Starter is fine.
- If you write for **several clients and want each one's brand profile, audits and AI-visibility tracking**, you need Professional (5 domains) or more.
- Research, briefs and drafts are the core of most freelance work. They count against your **article and research allowance**, not a per-client fee. Our trial allowed 15 research runs. Check how your plan's limits fit your volume before committing.

Two onboarding gotchas from our test:

- Frase needs a **root domain**. A subpath site like `name.github.io/blog` failed to read.
- The research language and market **default to your browser's locale**. If your browser isn't set to English, switch both before running research for an English-speaking client.

## Pros and cons for freelancers

**Pros**

- One tool from research to scored draft. In our test, the full loop took under 15 minutes of machine time.
- Briefs show content gaps and sourced facts, which helps you pitch angles and justify the work to clients.
- GEO scoring and AI-visibility tracking give you a new service to sell.
- Predictable flat pricing. Every plan includes every feature.
- Reviewers on [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/197955/Frase/reviews/){: rel="nofollow" } rate it highly (4.8/5 when we checked). They often praise the briefs, ease of use and support.

**Cons**

- AI drafts are weak on E-E-A-T, so plan real editing time.
- "Improve scores" and "Rewrite" each use one of your monthly article credits.
- Starter's single domain and single seat are tight for multi-client work.
- Our draft listed some headings twice while it was still finishing. Check structure before you deliver.
- Some reviewers report occasional bugs, an AI writer that ignores instructions, and limited non-English support.

## FAQ

**Can I use Frase for multiple clients on Starter?**
You can research and draft any topic within your monthly allowance. Brand Hub, site audits and AI-visibility tracking are tied to sites, though, and Starter includes one domain. Professional raises that to five.

**Do clients see that I used Frase?**
Not automatically. You can export or share briefs and drafts. It's good practice to disclose AI assistance if your contract asks about it.

**Is the AI draft good enough to deliver as-is?**
In our test, no. Structure and AI-search readiness scored high, but E-E-A-T was 33%. Budget time for fact-checking, first-hand detail, images and links.

**Does the free trial need a card?**
Our trial ran for 7 days and allowed 5 articles, 15 research runs and 50 audit pages. Third-party sources disagree on whether a card is required, so check the signup page before you start.

**What's cheaper than Frase for freelancers?**
NeuronWriter starts at $23/month for content analyses and scoring. It's lighter on briefs and doesn't include AI-search tracking at entry.
