## Pricing: what you get per dollar

**Frase** is the cheaper way in. Starter costs $49 a month ($39 billed yearly) and includes 10 researched articles, AI Visibility on ChatGPT and Google AI, 50 audit pages, 1 seat and 1 domain. Professional ($129, or $103 yearly) jumps to 40 articles, 3 seats, 5 domains and adds Perplexity tracking. Scale ($299, or $239 yearly) gives 100 articles, 5 seats, 10 domains and adds Claude and Gemini. Our [Frase pricing guide](/frase-pricing/) explains which plan fits which workload.

![Frase plans shown at checkout: Starter, Professional and Scale](/img/frase/28_plans.jpg)

**Writesonic** prices its 2026 plans around AI-visibility prompts. Starter ($99 a month, $79 yearly) tracks 50 prompts and includes 15 AI articles and 10 site audits for 1 user and 1 project. Basic ($249, $199 yearly) doubles prompts to 100, with 25 articles and 2 users. Growth ($499, $399 yearly) tracks 200 prompts, adds sentiment analysis, 50 articles, 3 users and 2 projects. Enterprise is a custom quote and is where reviewers say the wider list of AI platforms and the full "Action Center" live.

**What this means:** if you count articles, Frase is far cheaper. Ten articles for $39 is about $3.90 each on yearly billing; Writesonic Starter works out to roughly $5.30 per article, and the gap widens on the middle tier (40 articles for $103 vs 25 for $199). If you count tracked prompts, Writesonic gives you much more monitoring at the entry level.

## Writing: research-first vs template-first

**Frase** builds every article from research. In our test, the research step took about 4–5 minutes and pulled recommended keywords with volume and CPC, what the top pages cover, content gaps competitors miss, People Also Ask questions and citable statistics with sources. You pin what you want, and Frase turns it into a brief in under a minute: title, word count, tone, audience, key facts with "verified" badges, a core argument and a section-by-section outline. The draft itself took about 3–4 minutes for roughly 1,500 words.

![Frase research results with keywords, headings, questions and evidence to pin](/img/frase/13_research_results.jpg)

**Writesonic** is known for its Article Writer, which does live web research and can produce long drafts in one run, plus a large template library for other copy (ads, emails, product descriptions). That breadth is useful if you write many kinds of marketing copy. The most common complaint in independent reviews is the same one we'd raise about any AI writer: drafts need real editing before they're publishable, and some users mention AI-detection flags.

For an affiliate blogger or freelance SEO writer, Frase's workflow maps more directly onto how a ranking article gets made. Its brief even has content types for Comparison, Alternatives, Tool Review and Listicle, the exact formats affiliate sites publish.

## Optimization scoring

This is where Frase pulls clearly ahead for writers. The editor shows three scores side by side. **SEO** compares your draft to the top results. **GEO** estimates how quotable it is for AI answers. **EEAT** checks author and experience signals. Our test draft scored **94% GEO, 78% SEO and 33% EEAT**.

![Frase GEO score panel with quotability, structure and citable-data checks](/img/frase/23_geo_score.jpg)

The low EEAT score is the useful part. Frase told us exactly what was missing: original media, an author bio, credentials and first-hand experience. That's where a human editor should spend time, and it's the part no AI tool can fake for you.

In the Writesonic GEO plans we could verify, the focus is on visibility reporting and recommended actions rather than a live content score in the editor. If you rely on a score while writing, test that specifically during Writesonic's trial.

## AI search visibility (GEO tracking)

This is Writesonic's home ground. Its plans are built around tracked prompts: 50 on Starter, 200 on Growth, across ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Overviews. Reviewers like the breadth of tracking at the entry tier. The criticisms we saw: citation detail can be shallow, there's no revenue attribution, and support is email-only on lower tiers. Note that some of those reviews come from competing tracking vendors, so weigh them accordingly.

**Frase** includes AI Visibility on every plan. In our trial it showed visibility %, share of voice, average position and sentiment against tracked competitors, grouped prompts by buyer stage (awareness, consideration, decision), and listed which sites AI answers cited. That last part is the actionable bit: those are the sites to pitch or the formats to copy. Its Recommendations tab turned gaps into tasks like "get into the articles AI quotes". The trial allowed 5 prompts, so Frase is lighter on monitoring volume. Read more in our [Frase AI Visibility guide](/frase-ai-visibility/).

![Frase AI Visibility dashboard with visibility, share of voice and citation sources](/img/frase/09_ai_visibility.jpg)

**Bottom line here:** if you need to watch 50+ prompts for a brand, Writesonic is built for it. If you need to see where you're missing and then *write the content that fixes it*, Frase connects tracking to drafting in one tool.

## Pros and cons

### Frase

**Pros:** lower entry price; research that gathers keywords, competitor headings, questions, content gaps and citable stats; detailed briefs; full drafts in minutes; SEO/GEO/EEAT scoring; AI Visibility on every plan with citation sources; more domains and cheaper extra seats; 7-day trial without a card.

### Writesonic

**Pros:** many tracked AI prompts even on Starter; GEO-first dashboard and recommended actions; long-form writer with live web research; large template library for non-SEO copy; 7-day trial without a card.

**Cons:** costs about twice as much as Frase at entry level; fewer articles per dollar; only 3 AI engines on self-serve plans; reviewers report drafts that need heavy editing; plans and pricing have changed several times, which makes old reviews misleading; extra users reportedly cost $50/month.
