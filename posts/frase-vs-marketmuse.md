## Pricing: published vs "book a demo"

This is the biggest practical difference for small buyers.

**Frase** shows its prices in the app and on its site. We confirmed them in the in-app checkout during our trial: Starter is $49 a month ($39 billed yearly) with 10 researched articles, 1 seat, 1 domain and 50 audit pages a month. Professional is $129 ($103 yearly) with 40 articles, 3 seats and 5 domains. Scale is $299 ($239 yearly) with 100 articles, 5 seats and 10 domains. Our [Frase pricing guide](/frase-pricing/) breaks down which plan fits which workload.

**MarketMuse** has been part of Siteimprove since late 2024, and since then paid tiers show a "Book a demo" button instead of a price. Directories still list old figures (roughly $99 to $499 a month), but reviewers who checked MarketMuse's pricing page in September and November 2026 found no dollar amounts, so we don't quote any. The free plan gives one user 10 queries a month with research tools, but no tracked topics, briefs or strategy documents. Reviewers report paid tiers named Optimize, Research and Strategy, with brief allowances growing by tier.

**What this means:** a freelancer can sign up for Frase tonight and know the exact bill. With MarketMuse you budget after a sales call, and the most common complaint in its Capterra reviews is price.

## Writing: Frase drafts, MarketMuse plans

**Frase** is built to get an article written. In our test, research took about 4–5 minutes, the brief under a minute, and the draft about 3–4 minutes for roughly 1,500 words. You can assign some sections to yourself and let Frase write the rest.

The brief is detailed: a title suggestion, target word count, tone, funnel stage, audience personas, key facts with verified-source badges, a core argument, "what makes this different" and a section-by-section outline with evidence to include.

![A Frase content brief with article details, key facts and outline](/img/frase/16_brief_ready.jpg)

**MarketMuse** is built around its topic model. Reviewers say it analyzes far more pages than the top 10–30 search results most tools use, and its briefs are among the most detailed in the category, with nine brief types that export to Google Docs or Word. It does offer AI help to turn a brief into a draft, but independent reviews consistently describe the output as a starting point for writers rather than a finished article.

If your bottleneck is producing drafts, Frase saves more hours. If your bottleneck is deciding *what* a big site should publish next, MarketMuse is the more strategic tool.

## Scoring: three scores vs one topic score

Frase splits optimization into three scores. **SEO** compares your draft to the top results. **GEO** estimates how quotable it is for AI answers. **EEAT** checks author and experience signals. Our test draft scored **94% GEO, 78% SEO and 33% EEAT**.

That low EEAT number is honest and useful. The AI draft was well structured, but it had no original media and a mostly empty author profile. Frase told us exactly what to add: an author bio, credentials and real experience.

![Frase GEO score with quotability, structure and citable-data checks](/img/frase/23_geo_score.jpg)

MarketMuse gives a content optimization score based on how fully your draft covers the topics in its model. Users praise how it reveals subtopics competitors cover. Some say the keyword suggestions can drift off-topic and the interface takes time to learn.

## Strategy and site planning

Here MarketMuse is stronger. Its whole design is about topic authority across your site: which subjects you own, where pages are thin, and which clusters to build next. For a publisher with thousands of URLs, that inventory-first view is the main reason to pay for it.

Frase has caught up somewhat for small sites. Its Site Audit found 500+ URLs on our demo domain and ranked issues by priority. Clusters maps topics, and Competitive Analysis compares your page against any URL or the top results. Audit pages are capped, though: 50 a month on Starter, 250 on Professional and 1,000 on Scale. That fits a 100-page blog much better than a 10,000-page media site.

## AI search visibility (GEO)

Frase includes AI Visibility on every plan: ChatGPT and Google AI on Starter, plus Perplexity on Professional, plus Claude and Gemini on Scale. In our trial it showed visibility %, share of voice, average position and sentiment against tracked competitors. It also listed which sites AI answers cited, and that's where outreach should go. More in our [Frase AI Visibility guide](/frase-ai-visibility/).

For MarketMuse, the plan limits we could verify cover topics, briefs and queries. We could not confirm AI-answer tracking as a listed feature in November 2026. If GEO tracking matters to you, ask about it in the demo.

## Pros and cons

### Frase

**Pros:** clear published pricing; full AI drafts in minutes; research that collects keywords, competitor headings, People Also Ask questions, content gaps and citable statistics; three-part SEO/GEO/EEAT scoring; AI-search tracking on all plans; 7-day trial without a card.

**Cons:** raw drafts are weak on EEAT and need real human experience added; article credits reset monthly, and "Improve scores" or "Rewrite" each use one; extra seats cost $29/month; audit-page limits are tight on Starter; content language defaults to your browser locale, so check it before running research.

### MarketMuse

**Pros:** deep topic modeling across many pages; strong content inventory and planning for large sites; many brief types with Word/Google Docs export; free plan to explore; support is praised in user reviews.

**Cons:** no public pricing for paid plans; price is the top complaint; reviewers mention a steep learning curve; AI drafting isn't its focus; we could not verify AI-search tracking.

## FAQ

**Is Frase cheaper than MarketMuse?**
Almost certainly for small teams. Frase starts at $39/month billed yearly ($49 monthly). MarketMuse publishes no paid prices and asks you to book a demo.

**Does MarketMuse have a free plan?**
Yes: one user and 10 queries a month, without tracked topics, briefs or strategy documents, according to reviews that checked it in late 2026.

**Can MarketMuse write full articles?**
It offers AI help to turn briefs into drafts. Reviewers describe the result as a starting point rather than a finished article. Frase writes the full draft section by section.

**Which is better for content strategy on a large site?**
MarketMuse. Its topic authority and content inventory are built for sites with thousands of pages. Frase's audits and clusters fit smaller sites better.

**Which tool helps with AI search (ChatGPT, Google AI)?**
Frase includes a GEO score in the editor and AI Visibility tracking on every plan. We couldn't verify an equivalent tracking feature in MarketMuse's plans.

**Is there a Frase free trial?**
Yes, 7 days with no card. The trial includes 5 articles, 15 research runs, 50 audit pages and 5 AI-visibility prompts.

**Can I use both?**
Some larger teams plan in MarketMuse and draft elsewhere. For a small team, paying for both rarely makes sense. Pick the one that solves your real bottleneck.
